# CEFR Language Competency Mapping Framework

This document outlines the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) competency hierarchy implemented across the UniLang curriculum and assessment engine.

## 1. Proficiency Level Taxonomy

| Level | CEFR Designation | Target Active Vocabulary | Syntactic Structures | Communicative Capability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | Breakthrough / Beginner | 500 – 800 words | Basic SVO, present indicative, simple questions | Can understand familiar everyday expressions and basic phrases aimed at the satisfaction of concrete needs. |
| **A2** | Waystage / Elementary | 1,200 – 1,800 words | Compound sentences, past simple, basic modals, reflexive verbs | Can communicate in routine tasks requiring a direct exchange of information on familiar matters. |
| **B1** | Threshold / Intermediate | 2,500 – 3,500 words | Relative clauses, subjunctive basics, conditional tenses, passive voice | Can deal with most situations likely to arise whilst travelling and produce connected text on topics of personal interest. |
| **B2** | Vantage / Upper Intermediate | 4,000 – 6,000 words | Complex conditionals, indirect discourse, idiomatic expressions | Can understand the main ideas of complex text and interact with a degree of fluency with native speakers. |
| **C1** | Effective Operational | 8,000+ words | Stylistic nuances, academic registers, advanced rhetorical devices | Can express ideas fluently and spontaneously without much obvious searching for expressions. |

## 2. Assessment Calibration

- **Placement Engine:** Multi-stage adaptive test combining vocabulary recognition (Zipfian frequency brackets), syntax ordering, and cloze listening comprehension.
- **Mastery Criteria:** Minimum 85% accuracy on active production exercises and 90% retention rate across 3 consecutive SRS spaced repetitions before unlocking subsequent proficiency tiers.
